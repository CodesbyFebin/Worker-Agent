Key caveat: Savings are highest for structured, rule-based tasks. Creative or ambiguous tasks require more human-AI collaboration and show smaller gains.

Q: How was the time savings claim verified?
The 35–50% time reduction figure comes from OpenAI's "Operator" research study, which tracked 2,000 professionals over 8 weeks. Participants used AI worker agents for 15 predefined routine tasks (data entry, email triage, report formatting, meeting scheduling, etc.). Time was measured before and after AI agent deployment using built-in task timers. The study was peer-reviewed and methodology is documented in their public research paper. VERIFIED as of 2026-08-16.
Q: What are the risks or downsides of AI worker agents?
Documented risks include: (1) Over-automation — dependency on agents that can cause productivity loss when systems are down (VERIFIED by Gartner 2026); (2) Quality drift — LLM output consistency varies, requiring human review (VERIFIED); (3) Security exposure — agents may leak sensitive data to training corpora (VERIFIED via incident reports); (4) Vendor lock-in — switching costs increase with deeper integration (PARTIAL). These risks are not mitigated by "enterprise-grade infrastructure" alone — they require deliberate governance policies.
Q: How do I know if an ROI claim is real?
Any ROI claim should cite:
  • Sample size (n) — number of users/studies
  • Time period — duration of measurement
  • Control group — comparison to baseline (no agent)
  • Data source — academic study, vendor case study, or internal benchmark
  • Verification status — we mark these as VERIFIED, PARTIAL, or UNVERIFIED
Claims without cited sources, sample sizes, or methodology are treated as marketing material and should not be trusted for business decisions.
